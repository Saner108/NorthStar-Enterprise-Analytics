# STAKEHOLDER REGISTER

Version: 1.0

Project:
NorthStar Retail Group
Inventory Visibility Pilot — Electronics & Home Goods, Southwest Region

Status: Active
Scope Note: This register is scaled to match the pilot Charter (4-month, solo-analyst, single-region/category engagement). Stakeholders are used partly as real project sponsors and partly as roleplay personas for practicing requirements-gathering and executive communication — both uses are noted below.

---

# Purpose

Identifies who has interest in, influence over, or a role within the pilot. Kept intentionally small to match the actual scale of the engagement — a full enterprise steering committee would be disproportionate for a 20-store, single-category pilot run by one analyst.

---

# Pilot Stakeholders

## Chief Operating Officer (Roleplay — Executive Sponsor)

Role in Pilot

Approves the pilot's existence and reviews final findings. Not involved in day-to-day work.

Primary Interest

Whether the pilot approach is worth scaling enterprise-wide.

Engagement Cadence

Two checkpoints only: pilot kickoff approval, and final findings review at end of engagement (~4 months). No recurring meetings — a full 4-month pilot does not warrant monthly COO time.

Success Looks Like

A clear, evidence-based recommendation on whether to scale the pilot's approach.

---

## VP of Operations, Southwest Region (Roleplay — Business Sponsor)

Role in Pilot

Represents the operational perspective — the person who actually cares whether stockouts get fixed. Used as the primary roleplay persona for business requirements interviews.

Primary Interest

Reducing lost sales from stockouts in Southwest Electronics/Home Goods stores.

Key Questions This Stakeholder Would Ask

- "Which of my stores are worst affected?"
- "Is this costing us real money or is it a minor issue?"
- "Can I see this without waiting on a report from someone else?"

Engagement Cadence

Simulated biweekly check-in (used as a structured moment to test whether current findings would make sense to a real operations leader).

Success Looks Like

Can look at the dashboard and immediately understand which stores need attention.

---

## Junior Data Analyst (You — Project Lead)

Role in Pilot

Owns everything: requirements gathering, data modeling, SQL development, Python analysis, dashboard build, documentation, and executive communication.

Success Measures

- Every deliverable can be explained without notes
- Every KPI has a documented business justification
- Data model is technically sound and reproducible

---

## Southwest Regional Store Managers (Roleplay — Primary End Users)

Role in Pilot

Represent the people who would actually use a store-level dashboard day to day. Used to sanity-check that dashboard design is usable by a non-technical, time-constrained store-level audience — not just impressive to look at.

Primary Interest

A fast, simple way to see their own store's stock status without calling the DC.

Engagement Cadence

Simulated feedback pass during dashboard design (Phase 3/4) — used to pressure-test usability decisions.

---

## Southwest Distribution Center Manager (Roleplay — Data Source Context)

Role in Pilot

Represents the operational reality behind the Warehouse Management System data — used when reasoning about data quality assumptions, transfer logic, and realistic inventory movement patterns.

Primary Interest

Whether the analysis reflects how inventory actually moves in practice (not an oversimplified model).

Engagement Cadence

Consulted conceptually during Data Discovery (Phase 2) when defining data generation assumptions.

---

# What Was Deliberately Excluded

To keep this register honest and proportionate to a solo 4-month pilot, the following roles from the full enterprise version of this project are **not included** here: CEO, CFO, CIO, CMO, Director of Business Intelligence, Director of Data Engineering, Finance Director, Supply Chain Director (enterprise-wide), E-Commerce Director, HR, and Marketing. These roles are appropriate for the full $2M enterprise initiative referenced in the Company Profile, but including them here would misrepresent the actual scale of this engagement.

---

# Stakeholder Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Roleplay personas drift into unrealistic expectations (e.g., demanding enterprise-scale features) | Anchor every roleplay check-in back to the Charter's explicit pilot scope |
| Solo analyst has no one to validate findings against | Use structured self-review against the Charter's Definition of Success and the five quality standards (Business Value, Technical Accuracy, Communication, Professionalism, Interview Readiness) |

---

# Definition of Success

The stakeholder approach is successful when every deliverable can be justified against at least one real stakeholder need documented here — not built simply because a template listed it.
